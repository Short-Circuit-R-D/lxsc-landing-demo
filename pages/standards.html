Compliance you can explain

Studio compares every workplane with the target for its activity and returns a clear verdict, not just numbers.

Three ways to set a target

StandardSearch EN 12464-1 activities by keyword or category, filter by standard and edition, and select one. Its maintained illuminance (Em) and minimum uniformity (Uo) fill in.
CustomEnter your own target illuminance and minimum uniformity when the project follows a client brief rather than a standard.
NoneCalculate without a target and read the measured values directly. Automated studies need a standard or custom target.

How each result is judged

PassAverage illuminance and uniformity meet the target and stay within the overdesign cap.
Not recommendedThe target is met, but average illuminance is above the overdesign cap, which is 30% over the target by default and can be set from 0% to 200%.
Non-compliantAverage illuminance is below the target, uniformity is below the minimum, or both. A message names the criterion that failed.
No standardNo target is set, so values are measured without a pass or fail.

Where and how the light is measured

Task workplanesIlluminance is evaluated on a horizontal plane at a height above the finished floor. Add several, such as a desk plane and a floor-level circulation plane. Each is judged on its own.
Wall zoneA perimeter margin, 0.50 m by default, keeps shadows near the walls from skewing the uniformity of the task area.
Surface reflectancesCeiling, walls and floor start at 0.70, 0.50 and 0.20. Adjust them to match your finishes.
Maintenance and inter-reflectionsThe maintenance factor (0.80 by default) allows for lumen depreciation and dirt, and radiosity bounces (3 by default) account for light reflected between surfaces.
Power densityLighting power density in W/m² is reported beside the total connected load.

Glare (UGR) and color rendering (Ra) values are shown for reference when you browse standards in the wizard. The compliance audit evaluates maintained illuminance, uniformity and the overdesign cap.

LuxScale is a calculation tool. The designer remains responsible for the final design and for any additional requirements of the project.
